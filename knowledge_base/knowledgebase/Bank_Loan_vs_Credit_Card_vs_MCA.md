# Bank Loan & Credit Product & MCA Comparison 

This document is formatted for training AI agents. It compares **three financial products**:  
1. A bank-style amortizing loan  
2. A factor-rate merchant cash advance (MCA)  
3. A credit card debt scenario ($10,000 at 30% APR with $300 payments)

---

## Numbers Side-by-Side

## Bank Loan (67 mo, ~14.2% APR)
- **Loan/Balance Amount:** $80,000  
- **Total Payback:** ~$113,857  
- **Total Interest/Cost:** ~$33,857  
- **Monthly Payment:** ~$1,699  
- **APR Equivalent:** ~14.2%  
- **Time to Funding:** Up to 6 months  
- **Approval Odds:** Low (strict underwriting)  
- **Impact of Early Payoff:** Save interest  

---

## Factor Rate Loan (36 mo, 1.434 factor)
- **Loan/Balance Amount:** $80,000  
- **Total Payback:** $114,720  
- **Total Interest/Cost:** $34,720  
- **Monthly Payment:** ~$3,186.67 (daily/weekly possible)  
- **APR Equivalent:** ~25.14%  
- **Time to Funding:** Same day  
- **Approval Odds:** High (lenient underwriting)  
- **Impact of Early Payoff:** Case-by-case discount possible

## Credit Card Debt (6yr, $300/mo @ 30% APR)
- **Loan/Balance Amount:** $10,000  
- **Total Payback:** ~$22,265  
- **Total Interest/Cost:** ~$12,265  
- **Monthly Payment:** $300  
- **APR Equivalent:** ~30% APR  
- **Time to Funding:** Revolving — balance already owed  
- **Approval Odds:** Medium  
- **Impact of Early Payoff:** Paying more per month reduces cost  

---

## Main Differences Explained for a Borrower

### 1. Cost of Capital
- **Bank Loan:** Lower APR, lower monthly payments. Total cost is significant but spread out.  
- **Factor Rate Loan:** Higher APR due to shorter term and frequent payments. Total dollar cost ends up similar to the bank loan.  
- **Credit Card Debt ($300 payments):** Very high effective cost — total interest (≈122.7% of principal) exceeds the amount borrowed. Small monthly payments relative to APR lead to extended payoff and high costs.

### 2. Speed & Approval
- **Bank Loan:** Slow process (months), strict documentation, harder approvals.  
- **Factor Rate Loan:** Fast funding (same day possible), much easier approvals.  
- **Credit Card Debt:** Revolving line — funds are instantly available when charged. Fair chance of approval, but costly if carried longer term.

### 3. Flexibility
- **Bank Loan:** Restrictive, subject to covenants and oversight.  
- **Factor Rate Loan:** Flexible use, fewer restrictions, but rigid repayment obligations.  
- **Credit Card Debt:** Maximum flexibility — can use for purchases at will. But minimum payments trap borrowers in costly, drawn-out repayment.

---

## How to Explain to a Customer

**Bank Loan Analogy:** Like getting a mortgage. Slow approval, intense process, only well-qualified applicants succeed. Payments are smaller, term is longer, total cost is lower in APR terms.  

**Factor Rate Loan Analogy:** Like a fixed-price contract. You know the total upfront, payments are higher and possibly daily/weekly, but funding is extremely fast and approval is much easier. A great product if your ROI is known.  

**Credit Card Debt Analogy:** Like a revolving tab at a store. Easy to use anytime, but small fixed payments relative to interest trap you into long-term debt. At $300/month on $10,000 at 30% APR, it takes ~75 months to pay off and costs ~$12,265 in interest.  

---

## Key Takeaway

- **Bank Loan:** Best for qualified borrowers who can wait and want lowest APR.  
- **Factor Rate Loan:** Best for speed and accessibility, despite higher APR.  
- **Credit Card Debt:** Most flexible but most dangerous if balances are not aggressively paid down.
