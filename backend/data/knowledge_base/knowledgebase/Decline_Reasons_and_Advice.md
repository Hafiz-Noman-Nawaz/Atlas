## Decline Reasons and Advice Answer for Client
1. Active Tax Liens, Judgments, or Litigation
Answer: Settle or establish a payment plan for any active lawsuits, judgments, or liens, as lenders require a clear legal standing.

2. Credit Quality (Low FICO or Derogatory Trade Lines)
Answer: Review your personal and business credit reports for errors, pay down existing balances, and ensure all payments are made on time to boost your score.

3. Data Merch Match (Previous default/slow pay reported)
Answer: Resolve any outstanding defaults, judgments, or collections on your record and obtain a satisfaction or settlement letter before reapplying.

4. Excessive Negative Days, NSFs, or Overdrafts
Answer: Keep a positive daily bank balance and avoid overdrafts or non-sufficient funds (NSF) fees for the next 90 days.

5. Insufficient Time in Business
Answer: Keep your business account active and maintain positive balances. Most lenders require at least 6 months of continuous operation and banking history.

6. Insufficient, Inconsistent, or Declining Revenue/Cash Flow
Answer: This may be because of transfers or deposits that do not count as business revenue, or revenue is just below certain thresholds, so focus on increasing your monthly sales volume and maintaining consistent deposits over the next 3 months to demonstrate stable cash flow. 

7. Low Average Daily Balance
Answer: Leave a larger cushion in your business checking account at the end of each day. Lenders want to see your account isn't zeroing out.

8. Outside Lender's General Risk Parameters
Answer: Focus on improving your overall business health—increase daily balances, avoid overdrafts, and pay down existing debt to fit better into standard underwriting models.

9. Overleveraged / Too Many Existing Positions / Debt Relief
Answer: Pay down or consolidate your existing advances to lower your current daily/weekly payment obligations before taking on new debt.

10. Previous Default, Delinquency, or Collections
Answer: Resolve any outstanding defaults, judgments, or collections on your record and obtain a satisfaction or settlement letter before reapplying.

11. Restricted Industry
Answer: Because your specific industry is on this lender's restricted list, we will need to match you with a specialized funder who actively underwrites your sector.

12. Unable to Verify Identity or Business
Answer: Ensure your Secretary of State filings are active, your business address is verifiable, and you can provide clear government-issued IDs and voided checks.

13. Unverifiable Revenue / Suspicious Documentation / Online Banks
Answer: Transition your primary business transactions to a traditional business checking account to ensure all revenue is verifiable by lenders.

14. Background / criminal history
Answer: Background-related declines can be anything from criminal history, gambling, debt settlements etc so removing negative background data will resolve this issue

15. Bankruptcy
Answer: Client does not meet criteria of lenders willing to fund bankruptcy background so improving cash flow for 90 days with increasing deposits from customers and having no negative days, and good daily average balances to demonstrate confidence in payment affordability

16. Business not in good standing
Answer: Resolve any state-registration, filing, or good-standing issues before going back out. A clean SOS status and updated corporate records can materially improve approval odds on the next pass.

17. Current loan outstanding / existing financing
Answer: Lenders are telling you the business is already carrying too much live debt. Over the next 90 days, reduce outstanding balances, avoid adding new debt, and show the business can handle current obligations without stressing the operating account.

18. Declining revenue / downward sales trend
Answer: Lenders want to see the trend flatten or improve before they step back in. Hold off for at least 90 days and resubmit only once the newest statements show revenue has stabilized and is moving in the right direction.

19. Entity type / NAICS / business type mismatch
Answer: Make sure the entity documents, application, bank statements, and licensing all line up the same way before you resubmit. If the business structure is unusual, target lenders that are comfortable with that entity type and NAICS classification.

20. Fraud / doctored or manipulated statements
Answer: This is a serious issue and most lenders will not revisit the file unless the concern is fully resolved. Do not resubmit until you can provide clean source documents and a credible explanation that addresses the discrepancy head-on.

21. General underwriting / policy decline
Answer: This is a broad policy decline, which means the file needs to be stronger across the board before coming back. Spend the next 90 days improving cash flow, cleaning up bank activity, and resolving any credit or leverage issues so the overall profile underwrites better.

22. Identity / SSN verification issue
Answer: Identity mismatches are usually fixable, but they need to be cleaned up before the next submission. Verify the owner SSN, DOB, address, and legal business details now so the next application can pass validation cleanly.

23. Inconsistent or volatile revenue
Answer: The issue here is volatility, not just volume. Over the next 90 days, keep deposits more consistent month to month and avoid large swings that make the business look unpredictable to underwriting.

24. Internal risk / model score below threshold
Answer: Model-driven declines usually improve only when the underlying file gets stronger. Use the next 90 days to improve deposits, balances, and credit quality together so the lender's internal score recalculates more favorably.

25. Limited time in business
Answer: This business likely just needs more seasoning. Let the company and operating account age another 90 days, keep revenue stable, and come back with newer statements that prove the business is established and active.

26. Low deposit count / limited account activity
Answer: Lenders like to see frequent, recurring business activity rather than a few isolated credits. Over the next 90 days, increase the number of true business deposits and reduce dependence on one-off or transfer-based inflows.

27. Low deposit volume / low sales deposits
Answer: Even if sales exist, the deposits hitting the operating account are not strong enough for this lender. Improve deposit volume for at least three consecutive statement cycles and keep business receivables flowing into one primary bank account.

28. Poor payment history / modified or lowered payments
Answer: Lenders want to see current obligations performing cleanly. Spend the next 90 days getting every position current, coming off modified terms if possible, and producing statements that show no missed or returned payments.

29. Previous default / charge-off / collections history
Answer: Defaults and collection history are major red flags in this space. The best path over the next 90 days is to resolve or settle outstanding issues, document any payoff or workout agreements, and show perfect current payment behavior.

30. Proof of ownership / business verification / online presence
Answer: Lenders want to confirm the business is real, active, and controlled by the applicant. Use the next 90 days to tighten online presence, update public records, and have ownership documents ready before the next submission.

31. Recent decline / duplicate submission
Answer: Wait at least 90 days before resubmitting to the same lender and avoid sending the file to multiple shops at once. Use that time to fix the original issue and come back with fresh statements, a cleaner credit profile, and a tighter story.

32. Recent delinquencies / late pays / past due accounts
Answer: Recent delinquencies tell lenders the business is under immediate stress. The next 90 days should be about bringing everything current, stopping new late pays, and letting the file age into a cleaner recent payment record.

33. Recent funding / too soon since last advance
Answer: Many lenders will not stack immediately after another advance funds. Let the existing deal season for 60-90 days, keep payments current, and show the new capital actually improved revenue and balances before reapplying.

34. Restricted or ineligible bank / bank account type
Answer: Lenders want a stable, eligible business checking account with clean history. Keep one primary business account open and active for the next 90 days, avoid switching banks, and make sure statements are clearly in the business name.

35. Short-term hold / willing to reconsider later
Answer: Treat this like a temporary pause rather than a hard no. Over the next 90 days, focus on cleaner bank activity, improving revenue stability, and resolving any outstanding lender questions before reapplying.

36. Underwriter discretion / management not interested
Answer: When a file dies at final discretion, the best answer is a meaningfully better resubmission. Let 90 days pass, tighten the narrative, and come back only after the statements, balances, and debt picture are clearly stronger.

37. Weak credit profile / limited credit history
Answer: This file needs a cleaner overall credit profile, not just a higher score. Over the next 90 days, reduce utilization, avoid new inquiries, resolve derogatories where possible, and make sure the business and owner credit files report consistently.
